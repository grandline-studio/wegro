# Studying references without copying

Do reference research only when the user supplies references, asks for exploration, or the brief is open-ended Tier 2 work with no brand to anchor it. Browsing galleries costs time and tokens, and it pulls toward whatever the gallery currently features, which is another source of sameness.

## Process

1. **Start from the brief, not the gallery.** Write down the quality you're looking for ("calm authority for a public-sector form", "dense but friendly for warehouse staff") before looking.
2. **Collect from varied sources.** Gather three to six references, ideally in *different* visual languages and including at least one from outside the web: print, signage, packaging, instruments, maps, the domain's own documents. A single source produces a clone.
3. **Extract principles, not surfaces.** For each reference, fill in:

   | Reference | What works | Why it works (mechanism) | How it translates to *this* brief |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | e.g. a railway timetable | Dense data is legible | Strict column alignment, tabular figures, two weights only | Job list uses fixed columns and tabular times; status by weight + icon |

4. **Combine and transform.** The direction should come from several principles applied to this product's content. If someone could identify the single source by looking, you've copied it.
5. **Check before building.** Remove anything that is a reference's *signature*: its logo or mark, illustration style, distinctive layout, copy, photography, or exact palette and type pairing.

## Hard lines

- Never reproduce another site's code, copy, images, illustrations, icons, or brand assets.
- Don't recreate a specific site's layout pixel for pixel, even "as a starting point".
- Respect font licences: a typeface seen on a reference site may not be licensed for this project.

## Where to look (when needed)

- **The domain itself:** the product's physical world, its documents, competitors' *weaknesses*, the users' current tools.
- **Product and interaction patterns:** Mobbin (mobbin.com) and Page Flows (pageflows.com) for flows and states.
- **Web galleries** (use sparingly, and look across them): Awwwards, Siteinspire, Godly, Land-book, Httpster.
- **Typography:** foundry specimen sites, Google Fonts' and Fontshare's full catalogs (look past the top of the popularity sort).

Gallery availability and content change over time; these are pointers, not endorsements.
